DriverPass - System Analysis and Design Portfolio

## Project Summary
**DriverPass** is a software system designed for a local driving school client seeking to streamline and modernize their student enrollment, lesson scheduling, instructor management, and progress-tracking workflows. The system provides a centralized platform for students to book lessons and monitor their driving milestone progress, for instructors to manage availability and report student feedback, and for administrators to oversee operations, schedules, and reporting.

Reflection Questions & Answers

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
The client was a local driving school management team looking to replace manual or fragmented scheduling methods. They required an integrated software system that manages student registration, driving instructor schedules, lesson tracking, and administrative reporting efficiently and securely.

What did you do particularly well?
I excelled at translating high-level business goals and user pain points into precise, structured requirements. The resulting Business Requirements Document and System Design Document provide a clear blueprint that bridges functional needs with robust software architecture, utilizing thorough UML diagrams and process models.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
I would expand the exception handling and edge-case workflows—specifically around scheduling conflicts when multiple students attempt to book the same instructor slot simultaneously. I would incorporate detailed sequence diagrams and concurrency handling strategies to make the scheduling subsystem even more resilient.

How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
I analyzed the day-to-day pain points of students, instructors, and administrators to identify core functional requirements, translating them into use cases, data models, and system interactions. Considering user needs is crucial because software that fails to align with real-world user workflows results in low adoption, user frustration, and inefficiency, whereas user-centric design ensures high usability and operational success.

How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
My approach is iterative and structured, starting with comprehensive stakeholder analysis and requirements gathering before moving into object-oriented modeling and architecture design. In future projects, I plan to rely heavily on UML visual modeling (activity diagrams, class diagrams, and use case diagrams), iterative prototyping, and close stakeholder feedback loops to ensure alignment between business expectations and technical implementation.
